Team building

Tactics

Documentation

The court

The court editor is where you position your team. Pick a rotation with the numbered buttons at the top: rotations are named by where your setter stands, and the badge on each player is the court position they play that rotation. The court then shows your six on-court players at their base positions for the phase you have selected. Switch phase with the tabs (Serve, Receive, Attack, Defence); Defence adds Base, Left, Middle and Right sub-tabs, while Attack adds a Base sub-tab plus one for each of your attacking players.

Working with the court

Drag a player to move them: that sets where they stand for the phase and rotation you are viewing. Click a player to select them and open their instructions, where you set their attack priority, tempo, block and, on Receive, whether they pass the serve. Everything is per rotation, so step through all six.

The rotation table

The rotation table gives you quick access to all of your player settings for the current rotation. There are three sections: the reception, the attack and the block. Reception toggles whether you want that player to be participating in the reception of the serve or not. In the attack section you configure the priority and tempo for each player. The block section sets which kind of block you want that player to do and how to behave on each side. Toggling off a side means that player will not block on that side (a committed player will still chase and attempt to block their target).

Attack priority: Attack priority (High, Mid, Low or None) determines the likelihood of the setter sending the ball to the respective player. The priority algorithm takes into account all current priorities to determine the chances. A player on None is never set to. If every attacker is on None, the setter distributes equally, and those players attack from their default zone routes.

Substitutes: Every substitute appears below your starters, including your second libero. Give each one its own Can receive, attack priority, tempo and block: these apply the moment the substitute comes on court, overriding whoever they replace. A substitute must be designated to a starter before you can set its per-side block toggles, since only then is the rotation it plays known.

Base positions

A base position is where a player stands for a given phase of a rally. The match engine reads these to place your players when your team serves, receives, attacks and defends, so they are how you shape your court coverage. Keep in mind that switching to a new base is not instant on court: your players have to travel from where they were to the new spot, and there is not always time to get there, so do not place a player so far from their other positions that they cannot realistically reach it in time.

Serve and Receive

Serve is where your players stand while your team serves. Receive is your serve-receive formation, where they wait for the opponent's serve. On Receive you also choose who passes with each player's Can receive toggle: only the players you mark as receivers take the serve. More receivers cover more of the court and lower the chance of a no-touch ace, but there are trade-offs: receivers whose ranges overlap can get confused about who should take the ball and make mistakes, and a player who passes the serve has less time to set up their attack approach, so they attack less efficiently.

Attack bases

The Attack bases position your team on offense. Attack (Base) is where your players prepare for their approach, and where your setter goes to take the pass and set the ball. You will be able to set the attack routes for all your attacking players. When the setter sets, all attacking players will make an approach to either swing or feint. Keep in mind that players set to None will be available to cover. Next to the Attack Base, you will have one base for each attacking player. These bases allow you to position your non-attacking players to cover in case the attack is blocked or deflected. You will not be able to move any attacking players in these bases as they are considered to be at the end of their approach.

Defence bases

The Defence bases position your team while the opponent attacks. Defence (Base) is your neutral defensive shape. Left, Middle and Right are for the side the opponent attacks from: once the opponent sets, your team shifts to the matching side base to defend that point of attack. The Base defence matters most for the plays where there is no time to shift. On a setter dump, or a surprise first-touch attack such as an overpass, the opponent attacks before your team can read the point of attack, so your players defend from the neutral Defence base instead of a side base. On a dump only your back row can turn to dig the short ball, and they read it from exactly these positions, so a well-set Defence base is your answer to dumps and surprise attacks.

Player instructions

Click a player to open their instructions. Every instruction is set per rotation.

Can receive: whether this player passes the serve on Receive.

Attack priority: For each rotation, set player's attack priority: High, Mid, Low or None. The setter will pick the attacker based on those settings. multiple players can have the same priority, the engine will simply split the chances evenly with it's priority algorithm. A player set to None will not be an attack option for the setter and can instead be used to cover the attack. If at any point all the players in the court have None as their priority, the engine will give them all an equal chance to attack and use default attack routes for them.

Blocking instructions: On the Defence base you set each front-row blocker to Read or commit them to an opposing position (Outside Hitter, Middle Blocker, Opposite Hitter or Setter). This choice is made before the opponent sets, so it applies whoever they set to. A blocker who committed to the attacker the ball goes to forms a block against them much more easily.

Committing is a gamble. If the set goes to a different attacker, your committed blocker struggles to recover: against a high ball they have a reduced chance to join the block, and against a quick they cannot join at all unless that attacker is already right in front of them. A reading blocker takes no such risk.

On each defence side (Left, Middle, Right) you can toggle a front-row player between Block and No block. No block sits them out of the block for attacks from that side by other attackers, freeing them to cover and defend stray or unblocked spikes. A blocker you committed to an attacker still follows and blocks that attacker, even on a no-block side.

Set Tempo

You can also set the tempo preferences for each attacker: Quick or High. Quick attacks are much harder to form a block against but are really dependant on the setter skill to make the right pass and the hitter skill to connect. High attacks are much easier for the setter and attacker to connect, and at the same time are also easier to form a block against.

Setting a hitter to Quick tells your setter to attack fast to them whenever the pass is in system. A middle at the net runs a true quick. An outside or opposite at the net runs a fast set, lower and quicker than a high ball. A hitter attacking from the back row runs a fast pipe, a quick attack from behind the three-meter line.

Quick attacks are riskier for both the setter to nail and the hitter to connect. If the setter fails they will either set a high ball or miss the set completely.

The setter

The player marked Setter is the one setting that rotation, so they deliver the ball instead of attacking. If that setter is in the front row they still block, so they keep their blocking instruction. In a 6-2 the setter comes up from the back row to set while the other setter stays in the front row and hits as your opposite, so that front-row setter attacks like any hitter. In a 6-0 there is no fixed setter: whoever rotates to the setting spot sets that rally and everyone attacks.

Replaced by the libero

A back-row player set to Replaced by Libero has your libero on court in their place while they are in the back row, so they take no instructions during those rotations. They play as normal, attacking and blocking, in the rotations where they stay on in the front row. The one exception is serving: when the replaced player rotates to the serving spot they stay on to serve their turn, and the libero comes back on for them once they rotate off.

Default templates

The base-positions library gives you a ready-made template for each rotation system: Default 5-1, 6-2, 4-2 and 6-0. They are starting points you can apply and then adjust, and they cannot be deleted.

Saving and saved base presets

There are two different saves. The main Save button stores your whole tactics setup, the positions and every instruction, onto your team; this is what your team uses in matches. The Save inside the base-positions library saves only the court positions (the coordinates, who receives and the block tags) as a reusable base preset. Applying a preset loads it into the editor, but you still have to press the main Save to keep it on your team.

Sharing bases

From the base-positions library, Export saves a preset as a .bse file you can share. Anyone can Import that file, or paste a code, to add it to their own library. You can keep up to 16 of your own saved base presets.
