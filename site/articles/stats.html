Team building

Stats

Documentation

ATTAttack

Determines the efficiency of the player when spiking, dumping or sending the ball towards complicated places on the opposing side. The higher this stat, the higher the chances a given attack will score.

BLKBlock

Determines the likelihood of the player to react to the opposing setter's plays and position themselves to block the attack. It dictates the chances of a player to participate in a blocking group and the chances that the block touch will result in the ball going back towards the attacker's side.

SRVServe

The higher this stat, the better the player will be at serving and the better serve types they will be able to perform. A high serve stat increases the chances of aces and of the receiver making a mistake or passing the ball poorly.

RCVReception

The other defensive stat. It governs the player's ability to receive serves and attacks. The higher this stat, the higher the chances that the player will be able to react to tricky spikes and serves.

SETSetting

Determines the quality of the pass a spiker will get. Setting encompasses all stats related to making a good play. A high setting stat not only sets the spiker up for success but also reduces the chances of blockers reacting accordingly.

STAStamina

This stat governs your player's endurance. A low-stamina player will take less time to get exhausted, increasing the chances of them making mistakes. Low-stamina players also recover more slowly when on the bench. Coaches will be more likely to substitute an exhausted player based on your tiredness threshold setting.

Advanced stats guide Setting

The quality and consistency of a player's sets, and how well they can deliver quick-tempo sets.

Serve

How accurate and reliable a player's serve is, missing less and placing it better. High values also let them jump serve.

Spike

The strength and accuracy of a player's spikes: the higher it is, the harder and more precisely they hit.

Quick

How cleanly a player handles fast sets. For a hitter, it decides whether they connect on a quick, a fast set, or a fast pipe instead of committing an attack error. For a setter, it also helps them deliver those fast sets.

Power

How much pace a player generates: harder spikes off high sets, and faster, heavier serves.

Awareness

How well a player picks the smartest place to aim their attacks and serves, finding gaps and weak spots. It also drives how often a front-row setter dumps the ball.

Attack

Boosts the quality of a player's attack, making their spikes, dumps and serves have a higher chance to land better.

Back Attack

How well a player attacks from the back row, on a pipe or a back-row swing.

Reception

How well a player controls the ball on defense, both receiving serves and digging attacks.

Overhand

A player's overhand passing and hand-setting, used to receive float serves cleanly, to set the ball off a good pass, and to handle free balls and last-touch plays.

Bump

A player's forearm passing, used against hard, topspin serves, when digging, and to bump-set off a poor pass.

Block

How strong a player's block is at the net.

Read Block

How well a player reads the play to get up and time a block. This is the main blocking skill.

Commit Block

How reliably a blocker commits to an attacker when you set up dedicated blocking. It only applies with dedicated blocking.

Focus

How precisely a player lands their attacks and serves where they aim. It also helps them avoid mix-ups with teammates on defense. It also makes a blocker harder to fake out with a feint.

Defense

Boosts the quality of a player's defense, making their receptions, digs and blocks as clean as the situation allows.

Stamina

A player's endurance: how quickly they tire and how fast they recover on the bench. The more tired a player is, the more likely they are to make mistakes.

Reflex

A player's reach and range on defense: how far they can move to dig or receive, and how far they travel to help on a block. It also helps them react to surprise balls like dumps and tips.
