How injuries work

Documentation

Knocks

A knock is not an injury: it happens during a match and never lasts beyond it. The player is fully recovered by their next match. A hard knock forces the player out of the match; lighter knocks let them play on with reduced performance.

A knock can be one of three levels: shaken (plays on, slightly reduced), hurt (plays on, heavily reduced) or out (must leave the match at the next dead ball).

Playing while hurt not only reduces the player’s performance, but increases the chances of getting a long lasting injury.

Injuries

An injury takes the player out of the current match immediately and keeps them out for a number of real-world hours. It comes in four severities: Minor, Moderate, Severe and Critical. The more severe the injury, the rarer it is and the longer the recovery. The player cannot return until healed.

What drives the risk

Every action in a rally carries an injury risk, and some actions carry higher risks than others: spiking and blocking are the riskiest, jump serves and digs sit in the middle, and sets, serve receives, free balls and float serves carry the lowest risk.

That risk is then shaped by the player and your staff: older players get injured more, every player has a different injury-proneness, fatigue raises the risk as energy drains, and a better physiotherapist lowers it.

Recovery

Recovery time depends on the severity, the player’s age (older players heal slower) and your physician’s level (a better physician shortens it). Once the return time is passed, the player is fit again and plays at full strength. You are notified when a player gets injured and again when they recover.

Injured starters at match start

If a starter is still injured when a match starts, if the user configured their replacements properly in the Lineup, the replacements will take their place. The replacement inherits ALL of the starter’s settings: lineup slot, setter designation, attack preferences, designated sub entries, libero replacements and per-set variants. A bench backfill (only possible when the replacement came from the bench) is a reserve who takes the vacated bench seat and likewise inherits that seat’s settings.

Starters without a configured replacement are auto-filled from the bench only: their designated sub first, otherwise the best healthy bench player by role. Reserves are never promoted automatically: if no eligible bench player exists, the match is forfeited even if healthy reserves are available. These settings live inside each lineup preset, so switching presets switches your injury policy.

Knocks and injuries during a match

Knocks and injuries resolve at the next dead ball; the rally in which they happened always stands. A legal substitution is tried first (it counts against the substitution limit, preferring the designated sub); if none is possible, an exceptional substitution brings in any eligible off-court player beyond the limit. Either way the player is locked out for the rest of the match, and the substitute inherits the slot’s settings. A libero forced off is taken over by the second libero if configured; otherwise the team plays the rest of the match without a libero. If nobody can replace the player, the team is incomplete and forfeits the set.

Forfeits

A team that forfeits a set keeps the points it earned; the opponent is awarded the points needed to win the set. A team still incomplete at the next set forfeits the remaining sets and loses the match. A team that cannot field a full lineup at match load forfeits the whole match, with every set awarded to the opponent.

Where injuries can happen

Friendlies can knock a player out of that match but never cause an injury. The same is true for bot-controlled teams: only user-owned teams in real competitions accumulate injuries.

Medical staff

The physiotherapist reduces the chance of knocks and injuries as it levels up. The physician shortens recovery time as it levels up. Each staff card above shows its effect per level.
