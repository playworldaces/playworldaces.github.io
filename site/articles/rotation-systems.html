Rotation systems

Documentation

5-1 system

The 5-1 runs a single setter who sets in every rotation, front row and back. You get five attackers and the most consistent setting, at the cost of only two front-row attackers in the rotations where the setter is up front.

Pick your setter below. The player diagonally opposite becomes your opposite hitter, and the rest fill in as outsides and middles.

4-2 system

The 4-2 uses two setters positioned opposite each other, so one is always in the front row. The front-row setter takes the second ball, leaving two front-row attackers. Simple and stable, with fewer attacking options.

Pick one setter below. The player diagonally opposite is automatically your second setter. Whichever setter is in the front row sets that rotation.

6-2 system

The 6-2 uses two setters opposite each other; the one in the back row comes forward to set, so you always have three front-row attackers. The front-row setter hits as an opposite. The most offensive system, but it asks both setters to also attack.

Pick one setter below. The player diagonally opposite is automatically your second setter. The back-row setter comes forward to set while the front-row setter hits as the opposite.

6-0 system

The 6-0 has no designated setter. Whoever rotates into the setting position sets that rally, and everyone attacks. There is no fixed role, so no player is ever out of position. The simplest system and the neutral default.

There is nothing to assign. Every player sets when they rotate to the setting spot and hits otherwise. Offensive preferences still apply.
